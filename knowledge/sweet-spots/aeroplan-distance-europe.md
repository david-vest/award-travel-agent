Aeroplan partner awards are priced by the published origin/destination zone
and distance band, while Air Canada-operated awards can vary. Use the current
chart and actual routing mileage rather than assuming a city lies below a
particular threshold: connections can change the band and the price.

The distance bands apply to partner awards, so a shorter eligible itinerary
can cost less than a longer one. A stopover costs 5,000 points and has
separate routing restrictions; it is not a free extension.

The catch is that Aeroplan's own search tool does not clearly expose the
distance bands — it shows a price, not the underlying logic, so finding the
cheaper band usually means testing a few candidate cities rather than reading
it off a chart. Partner space must also be available on every segment of a
multi-city itinerary at once, which gets harder to find as stopovers are
added. Chase, Amex, Capital One, and Bilt all transfer to Aeroplan at roughly
1:1; Citi does not.

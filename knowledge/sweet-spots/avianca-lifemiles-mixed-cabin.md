LifeMiles has none of the round-trip or minimum-stay requirements that
revenue fares carry, and it prices one-way awards at exactly half the
round-trip cost with no penalty. That sounds unremarkable until it is
combined with LifeMiles' willingness to price a mixed-cabin itinerary — an
outbound flight in business and a return in economy, priced as two separate
one-ways rather than forcing a single cabin for the whole trip.

This is useful for a traveler who wants business class on the long overnight
leg out but is comfortable in economy on a shorter or better-timed return —
Chicago to Istanbul in business outbound, economy back, for example, prices
as the sum of the two one-way charts rather than a compromise round-trip
rate. It also means a one-way award to reposition for a separate trip is not
penalized the way many revenue one-ways are.

The tradeoff is that finding two independently bookable one-ways with saver
space on the desired dates takes more searching than a single round-trip
query, since each leg's availability is independent. LifeMiles' own site is
also not the most reliable place to confirm partner space — cross-check with
a search tool before assuming a route is open. Bilt, Capital One, Citi, and
Amex all transfer to LifeMiles at 1:1.

Airlines don't release the same amount of partner award space to every
outside program that can technically book them — an airline's own frequent
flyers, and sometimes specific favored partners, often see more saver-level
inventory than a search from an unrelated Star Alliance or Oneworld partner
turns up for the identical flight.

Lufthansa is the most-cited example of this. It has a longstanding
reputation for releasing noticeably less business-class saver space to
non-Miles&More partners — a United or Aeroplan search, for instance — than
what a Miles&More member sees searching directly on lufthansa.com for the
same flight. Singapore Airlines runs something similar, holding back some of
its "Saver" award inventory from Star Alliance partner searches while
showing more of it to KrisFlyer members directly.

The practical consequence is that a "no availability" result from one
program's search doesn't rule out the same flight actually having open
space — it may just mean that program isn't seeing what the operating
carrier's own members can. Cross-checking the operating airline's own
program, or a search tool that queries multiple sources rather than one
program's inventory view, before concluding a route is genuinely unbookable
is worth the extra step, especially on Lufthansa and Singapore.

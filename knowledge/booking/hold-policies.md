Free or low-cost holds let a traveler lock in scarce partner award space
while confirming trip details, and policy differs sharply by program — there
is no industry-standard answer to "can I hold this seat," so it's worth
checking the specific program rather than assuming.

United and Aeroplan do not publish a general free award-hold benefit. Do not
plan around an Alaska phone hold either unless an agent explicitly confirms it
for the exact itinerary: hold availability is not a reliable, published
benefit to treat as standard policy. If booking speculatively, compare the
current cancellation fee, refund timing, and any elite or credit-card waiver
before ticketing; those terms are not interchangeable with a true hold.

These policies are specific to the booking program rather than the
operating airline, and they change without much public notice, so a policy
that held a year ago is not a safe assumption today — checking the current
terms at the time of booking is worth the extra step. It's also worth
remembering that scarce partner award space, particularly in business or
first class, is not guaranteed to still be there after a hold period or a
delayed decision, so a hold reduces risk but doesn't eliminate it.

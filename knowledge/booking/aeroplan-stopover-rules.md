Aeroplan permits one stopover per direction of travel, including on a one-way
award. It costs 5,000 additional points, must be outside Canada and the U.S.,
and may last up to 45 days. It is not free and it is not unlimited by the
ticket's general validity. An open jaw can be used on an eligible itinerary,
but it cannot occur at the stopover point.

The stopover must be part of the original award reservation. Air Canada
states that stopovers can be booked online, so a phone call should be a
fallback for an itinerary that the site cannot price—not the assumed booking
path.

The bigger constraint is availability: every segment of a multi-city
itinerary needs partner saver space at the same time, and that gets
noticeably harder to find as more legs and stopovers are added. Building the
itinerary with flexible dates, and confirming space segment by segment
before committing to the full routing, avoids discovering late that one leg
has no seats left.

Carrier-imposed surcharges, often called "YQ," vary by which loyalty program
books the award, not just which airline actually operates the flight — the
same Lufthansa or ANA business-class seat can carry very different
out-of-pocket cash depending on the currency used to book it. This is one of
the most consequential and least obvious variables in award pricing, and it
can turn an apparently cheap mileage price into an expensive ticket once
fees are added.

British Airways and Iberia Avios, along with Virgin Atlantic Flying Club,
generally pass through the operating carrier's full surcharge, which can add
several hundred dollars each way on carriers like Lufthansa, Swiss, or ANA.
Aeroplan removed fuel surcharges from its own chart in a 2020 overhaul and
now charges close to government taxes and fees alone. LifeMiles, United
MileagePlus, and Alaska Mileage Plan also generally avoid adding
carrier-imposed surcharges beyond modest taxes and airport fees.

This is not a permanent property of a program — Aeroplan's own history shows
it can change — so confirm the actual taxes-and-fees total at checkout
before booking rather than relying on a currency's general reputation from a
prior year or a forum post that may be out of date.

"Married segments" is airline inventory logic that links two flights on one
ticket so they must be booked together at a single fare or award level, even
when each segment individually shows open saver-level space. It's one of the
more common causes of an award search tool displaying "Saver available" on
both legs of a connection, only for the actual booking to reprice at a
noticeably higher award level once combined.

This can affect United and other carriers, but it is principally an inventory
and availability control—not a rule that automatically prices a connection at
the higher of two individual awards. United's awards are dynamically priced,
so a combined itinerary can legitimately price differently for several
reasons. Do not diagnose every higher combined price as married-segment logic.

There is no reliable way to predict married-segment behavior from outside
the airline's own reservation system before attempting the booking — it
doesn't show up in a typical award search result. The practical workaround
is comparing the through itinerary, alternate connections, and separate
one-way tickets. Separate tickets can create missed-connection and baggage
risk, so they are not an automatic workaround.

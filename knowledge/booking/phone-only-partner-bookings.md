Several otherwise-attractive programs have partner-award booking engines
that are unreliable or incomplete online, which means a phone call is
frequently the realistic path to ticketing space that a search tool shows as
available. This is a common source of frustration: a traveler finds a seat,
tries to book it, and the website either fails to price it correctly or
drops the search entirely.

Turkish Miles&Smiles is the most cited example — its website frequently
fails to display or correctly price Star Alliance partner space that other
tools show as open. ANA Mileage Club's English-language site restricts some
partner routings that either the Japanese-language site or a phone agent can
complete. LifeMiles' website can show a seat as available and then fail to
actually ticket it at checkout, requiring a call to finish the booking.

Phone bookings usually require the account holder to call personally rather
than a companion or travel agent, hold times can run long outside a
program's peak staffing hours, and a phone agent's live availability view
does not always match what a search tool showed a few minutes earlier.
Having exact flight numbers, dates, and cabin class ready before calling —
rather than asking the agent to search from scratch — meaningfully shortens
the call and reduces the chance of losing the seat mid-conversation.

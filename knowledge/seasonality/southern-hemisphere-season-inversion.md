Peak travel demand in the Southern Hemisphere runs opposite the Northern
Hemisphere's calendar: December through February is summer, and the highest
domestic and inbound demand season, across South America, southern Africa,
and Australia and New Zealand. That's exactly the window when Northern
Hemisphere travelers have the most vacation flexibility from holidays and
school breaks to go there, and the overlap makes December-to-February award
space to destinations like Buenos Aires, Cape Town, Sydney, and Rio de
Janeiro scarce and expensive — comparable to how June-August behaves for
Europe from the US.

The Southern Hemisphere's own shoulder and low seasons — roughly April-May
and September-October, mirroring the North's — tend to have meaningfully
better award availability and lower cash-fare add-ons on the same routes,
making them a better target for a points-funded trip if the destination
itself doesn't demand a specific calendar window.

This inversion isn't total across every destination, though. Parts of
Brazil and other locations closer to the equator don't have as sharp a
seasonal demand swing as temperate destinations further south do, so it's
worth checking a specific destination's actual pattern rather than applying
the hemisphere rule uniformly to the whole region.

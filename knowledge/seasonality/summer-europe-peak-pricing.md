June through August is peak demand for transatlantic flights to Europe, and
programs whose charts are dynamically or partially dynamically priced —
United, Delta, American, and Aeroplan on some routes — reflect that with
meaningfully higher award prices in peak summer than the same route and
cabin would cost in a shoulder month. A Chicago-London business-class award
that prices reasonably in April can cost noticeably more for an otherwise
identical July date on a dynamically-priced chart.

Programs still running largely fixed award charts — LifeMiles and Turkish
Miles&Smiles among them — are comparatively insulated from the summer
markup in mileage terms, since their published prices don't move with
demand the way a dynamic chart does. That said, scarce saver-level space is
still the real constraint in peak summer regardless of chart type; a fixed
chart doesn't help if there's simply no award seat to buy at any price.

Dynamic pricing cuts both ways. It's not uniformly expensive across every
summer date — a specific date occasionally prices well below the usual range
if an airline is trying to fill inventory close to departure or on a
particular day of week. Comparing several dates a week or two apart, rather
than assuming the whole season is priced the same, is usually worth the
extra search time.

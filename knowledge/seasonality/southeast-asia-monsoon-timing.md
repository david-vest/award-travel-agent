Southeast Asia's monsoon patterns are regional rather than uniform, which
matters for picking dates on a multi-country award trip. Thailand, Vietnam,
and the Philippines see their wettest months roughly May through October
under the southwest monsoon, while Bali and the parts of Indonesia south of
the equator run on close to the opposite cycle, with their wetter season
roughly November through March. A single "avoid monsoon season" rule doesn't
work for an itinerary that combines, say, Bangkok and Bali — there is no one
dry-season window that suits both.

Award availability itself doesn't track the rain calendar closely. It's
driven far more by regional holiday demand — Lunar New Year and Japanese
Golden Week in particular — than by rainfall, so wet-season months are often
some of the easier times to find saver-level space on regional and
long-haul partner awards into the region.

"Rainy season" in most of Southeast Asia also tends to mean heavy but
short afternoon downpours rather than washed-out weeks of bad weather, so
it's a real consideration but frequently overstated as a trip-planning
deterrent. Weighing it against the better award availability and lower
cash-fare add-ons the off-peak months often bring is worth doing before
ruling out a wet-season date purely on the weather.

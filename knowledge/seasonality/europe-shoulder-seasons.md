April-May and September-October are the strongest shoulder-season windows
for a Europe redemption — weather is still workable across most of the
continent, cash fares and crowds drop from their peak, and award
availability opens up noticeably compared to June-August. Business-class
saver space that's essentially unavailable in July on major transatlantic
routes, including Chicago-London and Chicago-Frankfurt, tends to reappear by
mid-September.

Shoulder season isn't uniform across the continent, though. Southern Europe
— Greece, southern Italy, Spain — stays warm and busy into early October, so
its practical shoulder window runs later than Northern and Central Europe,
which cool off and quiet down earlier, closer to late August.

Airlines also adjust widebody frequency seasonally, which cuts against the
availability benefit somewhat: a route flown daily in peak summer might drop
to four or five times weekly by October, so there are fewer total flight
options to search even though the ones that remain have better award space.
Weighing both effects together — more available seats per flight, but fewer
flights — matters more than assuming shoulder season is unambiguously easier
to book across the board.

Japan has two demand peaks that make award space scarce well in advance:
cherry blossom season, roughly late March into early April, and autumn
foliage (koyo), roughly mid-November into early December, with both windows
shifting by region and by one to three weeks year to year depending on
weather. Business and first-class saver space on ANA, JAL, and United
nonstops from Chicago to Tokyo routinely disappears nine to eleven months
before travel for these dates, well before most travelers start planning.

Golden Week, roughly late April into early May, is a third peak, driven
mostly by domestic Japanese travel rather than inbound tourism, but it still
strains partner award space since Japanese travelers are flying heavily
during that window too.

The practical risk is that exact bloom and peak-foliage dates aren't
predictable more than about two weeks out, so booking to a single fixed
calendar date months in advance carries real uncertainty about whether it
will actually land during peak color. Travelers with date flexibility are
better served searching a range of dates rather than committing to one week,
and the more reliable strategy overall is booking as early as award space
opens — often around eleven to twelve months out — rather than waiting for a
confirmed seasonal forecast that won't arrive in time to help.

Lufthansa is rolling out a new long-haul business class suite, but a
meaningful share of its widebody fleet, including most A340-600s, still
flies the previous-generation seat: a 2-2-2 layout that is genuinely
lie-flat but positioned so that in an occupied pair, the window-seat
passenger has to climb over the aisle passenger to get up — there's no
direct aisle access from every seat, unlike the newer suite or most
competing Star Alliance business products.

The seat itself is a real step down in privacy and convenience from ANA's
Room or the newer Lufthansa suite, but it prices identically on Star
Alliance partner charts — Aeroplan, United, and LifeMiles all charge the
same mileage price for the A340's 2-2-2 seat as for a route flying the newer
product, so the aircraft matters more than the mileage cost when comparing
options on the same chart.

Lufthansa's food and service are consistently solid, if not flashy, across
its fleet regardless of seat generation. The practical takeaway is the same
one that applies across several other Star Alliance products in this
collection: check the specific aircraft and seat map for the actual flight
date before booking, since "Lufthansa business class" as a label covers two
meaningfully different products depending on which jet operates the route.

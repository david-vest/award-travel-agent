Turkish Airlines' business class on its 787-9s is a solid, modern 1-2-1
fully flat product, a real step up from the airline's older widebody
business seats, which still fly on part of the A330 and 777 fleet in a
dated 2-2-2 configuration without direct aisle access from every seat.

Turkish's real differentiator, though, is on the ground and in the galley
rather than the seat itself. Istanbul's business-class lounge is one of the
most elaborate in the industry — live cooking stations, a cinema room, and a
putting green among its features — and inflight catering, including a
dedicated "chef on board" service on select long-haul routes, is
consistently rated above most Star Alliance peers covered in this
collection.

Istanbul's hub position also makes Turkish a practical one-stop option from
Chicago to parts of Africa, Central Asia, and the Middle East that lack good
nonstop alternatives from the US. As with the airline's award-booking
friction described elsewhere in this knowledge base, matching this specific
seat to an actual flight takes some care: Istanbul-origin long-haul routes
skew toward the 787 and A350 fleet, but Turkish's US gateway flights,
including Chicago, sometimes still operate on older-configuration 777s, so
checking the aircraft before booking is worth the extra step.

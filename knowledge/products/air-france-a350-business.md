Air France's business class on the A350-900 is a 1-2-1 reverse-herringbone
seat with direct aisle access from every seat and a noticeably wider seat
than the airline's older 777 business product, which still flies a tighter
layout on part of the fleet depending on the specific jet's configuration.

Air France has been shifting more A350s onto long-haul US routes over time,
but Chicago itineraries still see a mix of aircraft depending on the season
and day of week, so the A350 isn't guaranteed on a given Chicago-Paris
date — checking the operating aircraft at booking is worth doing rather than
assuming the newest jet.

Air France's business-class dining and wine service is a genuine strength
relative to most Star Alliance and Oneworld peers, drawing on well-known
chefs for seasonal long-haul menus, and it's one of the more consistently
praised aspects of the product regardless of which aircraft operates the
flight.

The financial catch is surcharges: Flying Blue passes along carrier-imposed
fees on some Air France awards, which can meaningfully add to the
out-of-pocket cost even when the mileage price itself looks reasonable.
Checking the full taxes-and-fees total at checkout, not just the headline
mileage number, gives the real picture of what a specific redemption will
actually cost.

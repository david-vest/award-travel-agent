United's Polaris business class is a 1-1-1 fully flat, reverse-herringbone-
style seat with direct aisle access from every seat, but the actual
experience varies more by aircraft than the branding suggests. On the
767-300ER — a workhorse on several Chicago transatlantic routes — Polaris
seats are noticeably narrower and tighter than the same product on United's
777s or 787-9s and 787-10s, since the 767's narrower fuselage forces a
tighter 1-1-1 layout with less width per seat than the wider-body jets
allow.

Do not use the old non-flat “p.s.”-seat statement to evaluate a 767 award;
it is stale. Still check the specific flight's seat map and aircraft changes,
as equipment substitutions can alter the product and an airline's booking
display is not a guarantee of the final operating aircraft.

As with several other Star Alliance products in this collection, aircraft
type is a bigger variable than the airline name once the mileage price is
already fixed. A Polaris booking on a 767 delivers a real but tighter
version of the seat than the identical fare class on a 777 or 787, and
United's own seat map for the specific tail number — not just the booking
class shown at search — is the only reliable way to confirm which version a
traveler will actually get before flying.

Singapore Airlines' business class on the regionally-configured A350-900,
flown mostly on shorter Southeast Asia routes, is a well-regarded 1-2-1
reverse-herringbone seat — but it's a notch below the carrier's flagship
long-haul products. Singapore's true benchmark seats, its Suites cabin and
the extra-wide long-haul business class on its A380s and 777s, deliver
meaningfully more space and are reserved for the longer intercontinental
routes rather than the regional A350.

Singapore doesn't fly Chicago nonstop, so most Chicago-originating
itineraries connect through a partner hub or a Singapore gateway elsewhere
in the US. That means a traveler booking through to Southeast Asia can end
up on the regional A350 product for a connecting leg even when the main
transpacific segment is flown on a larger, better-equipped aircraft.

Singapore Airlines is a Star Alliance member, so its business class is
bookable through United MileagePlus, Aeroplan, and Singapore's own
KrisFlyer program, but pricing and availability differ noticeably between
them. Singapore tends to release relatively little Saver-level business
space to partner programs, so checking KrisFlyer's own availability directly
— even when planning to book with a different program's miles through a
search tool — is usually worth the extra step before assuming a route has no
space.

Qatar Airways' Qsuite is widely regarded as the benchmark business-class
product in the industry. Every seat has a sliding privacy door, and the
middle pair of seats can drop the divider between them to form either a
double bed or, combined with the row in front or behind, a four-person
suite for groups traveling together — a configuration essentially no other
airline offers at scale.

Qsuite flies on most of Qatar's A350s and a growing share of its 777 fleet,
though the retrofit isn't complete across every aircraft. Doha's hub
position also makes it a genuinely useful one-stop connection from the US
to South Asia, East Africa, and parts of the Middle East that don't have
practical nonstop alternatives from most US gateways, including Chicago.

The catch is availability. Qsuite award space, whether booked through
Qatar's own Privilege Club or a partner program like British Airways Avios,
is some of the most competitive in the industry — saver-level seats often
disappear within hours of being released. Non-Qsuite aircraft, flying older
777 business configurations, can also appear on the same route without much
warning, so confirming the specific aircraft before booking matters as much
here as it does with Lufthansa's mixed A340 fleet.

EVA Air's Royal Laurel business class on the 787 fleet is a staggered 1-2-1
fully-flat product with direct aisle access from every seat; it is not a
reverse-herringbone seat. It is a real step up from EVA's older 2-3-2
business product that still flies on part of the 777 fleet. EVA connects
Chicago to Taipei nonstop, which makes it a practical Star Alliance option
for Chicago-Asia travel alongside United and ANA, and it often has better
saver-level award availability than the higher-profile carriers on the same
partner chart.

Service is warm and detail-oriented by reputation, with a well-regarded
amenity kit, and EVA flies a small number of Hello Kitty-branded aircraft on
select routes that some travelers deliberately seek out or specifically
avoid depending on taste.

As with several other Star Alliance products covered here, the aircraft
matters more than the airline name when booking. EVA runs a mixed fleet
across its long-haul network, and a 777 award booked at the same mileage
price as a 787 award can deliver the noticeably inferior 2-3-2 layout
instead of the reverse-herringbone seat. Checking the specific aircraft type
for the actual flight date, not just the route, is worth the extra step
before committing miles.

A cents-per-point baseline is a quick sanity check for whether a specific
redemption is a good deal: divide the cash price of an equivalent ticket by
the miles-plus-fees required, and compare the result to what that currency
typically delivers. It should never be the final word — a specific
redemption should be judged on its own merits — but it's a fast way to spot
a bad trade before spending time building an itinerary.

There is no stable, universal cents-per-point value. A transferable bank point
has a different value depending on the transfer partner, award availability,
cash alternative, taxes, cancellation rules, and whether the traveler would
actually have paid the cash fare. Use the calculation as a comparison tool,
not a target to maximize. In particular, do not treat a high valuation of an
unavailable premium-cabin seat as real savings.

These ranges shift over time as programs devalue or introduce dynamic
pricing, so treat them as a rough baseline for comparing options against
each other, not a fixed table to look up.
